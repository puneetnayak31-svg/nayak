## HIPL — What We're Building

Oct 10, 2026 · @Puneet Nayak

HIPL (Hinduism in Public Life) does six things. Each one has a simple format, a regular rhythm and a home on the website. Launch dates are decided separately.

### At a glance

| # | What | In one line | How often | What people do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | HIPL Dialogues | Public debates on one hard question | Monthly | Attend, vote, ask a question |
| 2 | HIPL Interviews | One-to-one conversations with thinkers, practitioners and young voices | 2 a month | Watch, listen, suggest a guest |
| 3 | The First 100 Questions | People ask what they always wanted to ask; we answer | Always open | Ask, upvote, read answers |
| 4 | HIPL Explorations | Clear, sourced explainers on Hindu ideas, texts and stories | New entries monthly | Read, explore, share |
| 5 | The HIPL Reading Circle | One text, one question, one discussion | Monthly | Read along, join the session |
| 6 | The Weekly Dispatch | A newsletter that ties it all together | Every week | Subscribe, reply |

How they connect. A question from the 100 Questions can become a Dialogue, an Interview or an Exploration. The Dispatch carries the best of all six to people's inboxes every week. The website is where everything lives.

### 1. HIPL Dialogues — public debates

Once a month, people who genuinely disagree debate one hard question on stage, and the audience decides which arguments moved them.

Format (about 90 minutes)

- Audience votes on the question before the debate (agree / disagree / unsure).

- Two or three speakers per side make their case.

- Moderated back-and-forth between sides.

- Audience questions.

- Audience votes again; the shift in votes is announced.

First topics to choose from

- Can tradition evolve without losing its essence?

- Is Hinduism a religion, a philosophy or a way of life?

- Can Hindu society confront caste honestly?

- Is dharma relevant at work?

- Does social media bring us closer to our culture, or reduce it to aesthetics?

On the website: a page for each Dialogue with the question, speakers, date and venue, RSVP, a short background brief and a question box. After the event: the recording, the before-and-after vote, and a short summary.

Each Dialogue also gives us: 4–5 short clips for social media, one Dispatch issue and often a Reading Circle text.

### 2. HIPL Interviews — one-to-one conversations

Two in-depth, one-to-one conversations a month with people who live Hindu ideas in public life: scholars, artists, practitioners, entrepreneurs and young voices.

Format: 30–45 minutes on video (YouTube and podcast apps), one host and one guest, a single theme per episode. Each interview is cut into 3–5 short clips for Instagram and YouTube Shorts.

Who to invite

| Type of guest | Example angle |
|---|---|
| Scholar or writer | What do the texts actually say about dharma? |
| Artist or musician | How do you reinvent a tradition without losing it? |
| Practitioner (priest, teacher, seva worker) | What does faith look like in daily work? |
| Young creator or professional | What do people my age actually believe? |
| Elder | What has changed in 60 years, and what should not? |

A special recurring episode: once a month, an elder and a Gen Z guest answer the same questions side by side. It's easy to film, it shares well, and it shows two generations in conversation.

On the website: an Interviews page with the latest episode on top and filters by theme. Each episode page has the video, guest bio, 3–5 key moments with timestamps, a transcript, and links to related Explorations.

### 3. The First 100 Questions

Anyone can ask the question about Hinduism, culture or society they've always wanted to ask. We collect the first 100 and commit to answering them in public.

How it works

- People submit a question on the website (name optional).

- The team reviews every question before it is shown. Abuse and spam are removed, but hard and uncomfortable questions stay.

- Published questions are numbered (001/100) and grouped by theme: belief and self, texts and stories, society and caste, gender, ritual, science, public life.

- Each question is answered in the best format: a short video, an Exploration, an Interview, or a whole Dialogue.

- Each question shows its status: Received, Being answered or Answered, with a link to the answer.

Why it matters: it gives us content ideas straight from the audience, makes people feel heard, and makes a great social series ("Question 014 of 100").

On the website: a page with a big counter, the submit box, the list of questions with filters and statuses, and an upvote button.

### 4. HIPL Explorations — knowledge and information

A permanent library of clear, sourced, good-looking explainers on Hindu ideas, texts and stories. Each one is written once and kept up to date.

Three series

| Series | What it covers | First entries to make |
|---|---|---|
| The Hindu Universe | Core ideas, texts, schools and thinkers | Where Do You Even Begin? · Dharma: One Word, Many Meanings · The Bhagavad Gita · Karma |
| Stories That Stay | Great stories and dilemmas, with context | Arjuna's Dilemma · Nachiketa and the King of Death · Krishna and Sudama · One Ramayana, Many Ramayanas |
| Ancient Ideas, Modern Life | Hindu ideas meet today's questions | Dharma and Career Ambition · Grandparents, My Generation and Faith · Seva Today · Pluralism |

The full list of 36 entries is in the team's Explorations brief. Start with these 12 and add more each month.

Every entry has the same shape: a hook → the story or idea → 3–6 short chapters or cards → why it matters today → a question for the reader → sources and related content.

Three page types, not 36 custom pages: Explainer (cards and chapters), Illustrated story, and Interactive (a map, a timeline or a "What would you do?" dilemma).

Where facts come from

- Original texts and critical editions: the BORI critical edition of the Mahabharata, the Gita Supersite (Gita, Valmiki Ramayana, Upanishads and more, with classical commentaries) and GRETIL for Sanskrit texts.

- Good translations: the Murty Classical Library of India and university-press translations, always named.

- Scholarly reference: the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Brill's Encyclopedia of Hinduism and peer-reviewed books.

- Lived tradition: interviews with practitioners, labelled as one community's view.

- Never used as a source: blogs, WhatsApp forwards, quote sites, AI text. Wikipedia is a starting point only, never the citation.

Three ground rules: always say which text or tradition you mean, never invent a quote, and present different interpretations instead of one "correct" answer.

### 5. The HIPL Reading Circle

One text, one question, one conversation a month, held online and in person.

How it works

- At the start of the month we announce the text and one guiding question, along with a 2-page reading guide.

- People read along at their own pace. Texts should be short enough to finish in a month and freely or legally available.

- At the end of the month a facilitator leads a 60-minute discussion for 12–20 people, online and in one city.

- We publish a short summary of what the group discussed.

Example texts: a chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, the Katha Upanishad (Nachiketa), an essay by a modern Indian thinker, a Bhakti poet's verses, or the text behind that month's Dialogue.

On the website: the current text and question, a reading guide to download, session dates, a sign-up form, and an archive of past circles.

### 6. The Weekly Dispatch

One email every week, a 5-minute read, that people look forward to opening. It is our most direct line to the audience and ties all six things together.

Same six sections every week, in the same order

- One idea worth thinking about: a short essay of about 300 words.

- This week in Hindu culture and public life: 2–3 developments, explained calmly.

- One positive story: a person or community doing something good.

- From HIPL: the latest Dialogue, Interview, Exploration or answered question.

- Three things to read, watch or hear: good links from anywhere.

- One question for you: readers reply, and the best replies feature next week.

Rhythm: goes out every week on the same day and time (for example, Sunday 8 am). Every issue is also posted on the website.

On the website: a signup box on every page, a Dispatch page with a sample issue and the archive of past issues.

Rule: never skip a week. A shorter issue is better than a missing one.

### The website, simply

Eight pages in the menu: one for each of the six things, plus Home and About.

Menu: Dialogues · Interviews · 100 Questions · Explorations · Reading Circle · Dispatch · About, plus an Ask a question button.

Home page, top to bottom: big welcome with the tagline → next Dialogue → latest Interview → 100 Questions counter with an ask box → featured Explorations → this month's Reading Circle → Dispatch signup.

Basics the site must have

- Works beautifully on phones first.

- Easy for the team to update without a developer (a simple content admin).

- Forms for RSVPs, questions, Reading Circle sign-ups and the newsletter, with spam protection and clear consent.

- Fast, readable by everyone (accessible), and easy to find on Google.

- Ready for Hindi later.

- No logins, comments or forums for now.
