Arjuna's Dilemma

Two armies face each other. On the other side: teachers, cousins, elders. The greatest archer of his age puts down his bow.

Source: Bhagavad Gita, chapters 1–2, part of the Bhīṣma Parva of the Mahabharata (BORI critical edition).

The setup

On the field of Kurukṣetra, the Pāṇḍava and Kaurava armies are drawn up for war. Arjuna asks his charioteer, Krishna, to drive between the two armies so he can see who he must fight.

He sees grandfathers, teachers, uncles, brothers, sons and friends. Overcome with grief, he says he does not want victory at this price, and sits down in the chariot, his bow slipping from his hands.1

What would you do?

You are Arjuna. Everyone you love is on that field. What do you do?

No score and no correct answer. After you choose, you'll see a perspective on every option and what share of readers chose each. Sample shares

Fight

In chapter 2, Krishna argues that Arjuna should act according to his duty as a warrior (), act without attachment to results (Gita 2.47 VERIFY), and remember that the self does not die when the body does (Gita 2.20 VERIFY).

Walk away

These are Arjuna's own reasons in chapter 1: grief, the collapse of families, and the wrong of killing one's own kin. The Gita gives them full voice before answering them.

Negotiate again

The Mahabharata describes peace missions before the war, including Krishna's own, and they failed.2 When is compromise still possible, and when has that moment passed?

Treat it as an inner battle

An allegorical reading, made famous by Gandhi, sees the battlefield as the human heart, where the war is between our better and worse selves.

How great commentators read it

One conversation, many readings.

Readings · 1/4Śaṅkara

Knowledge frees

Reads the Gita through Advaita: action purifies the mind, but liberation comes from knowing the self.

Readings · 2/4Rāmānuja

Devotion and surrender

Reads it through Viśiṣṭādvaita: selfless action and loving devotion to a personal God lead to liberation.

Readings · 3/4Bhakti

Take refuge

Devotional readings centre the Gita's closing call to surrender to Krishna (Gita 18.66 VERIFY).

Readings · 4/4Modern

Action in the world

Modern readers such as Tilak and Gandhi read it as a guide to selfless action in public life, though they drew different conclusions.

Why it matters today

What do you owe, when every choice costs something?

Most of us won't face a battlefield. But we do face choices where every option hurts someone: a family business, a whistle to blow, a friendship to end. Arjuna's dilemma is a model for thinking those through slowly and honestly. Which of the four answers do you find hardest to dismiss?
