Background brief

What is being argued, in two minutes.

Every living tradition changes: its languages, its festivals, who leads its rituals, how its stories are told. The question is whether some things must stay fixed for the tradition to still be itself, and who decides what those things are.

The case for "yes". Retelling and reform are part of the tradition, not a threat to it. Many traditions have adapted their practice across regions and centuries while keeping their core questions alive.

The case for "not without loss". Change is rarely neutral. When practices are simplified for convenience or reshaped for the market, something learned over generations can disappear quietly, and no one may notice until it is gone.
