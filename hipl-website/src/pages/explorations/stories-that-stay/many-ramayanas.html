Many tellings

Most people meet the Ramayana as one story: Rāma's exile, Sītā's abduction, the war with Rāvaṇa and the return home. But the story has been told in Sanskrit and in dozens of other languages, in poetry, song, painting, dance and shadow-puppet theatre, across South and Southeast Asia.

Each telling names its own version. "The Ramayana" usually means Vālmīki's Sanskrit text, but Tulsidas's Rāmcharitmānas or Kamban's Rāmāvatāram are just as central to the communities who read them.

Why it matters today

A story can belong to many communities at once.

Retelling is part of the tradition, not a threat to it. When someone says "that's not how the Ramayana goes", the honest reply is often "which one?" A widely cited essay by A. K. Ramanujan, "Three Hundred Ramayanas", makes this case; it is also contested, and we present it as one scholarly view.
